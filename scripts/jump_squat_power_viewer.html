Axial compression / distraction / shear

Reaction on the distal body is resolved along a bone long axis that rotates with the pose. Positive axial force is compressive, negative is distractive; the perpendicular remainder is shear. Shoulder and wrist long-axis components are not identified articular-surface normal forces. The video still shows total reaction magnitude, with black-to-red colouring.

These ideal-joint resultants do not identify cartilage pressure or individual ligament tension. Their magnitudes also depend on the selected muscle-force solution.

Normalized tension: model review

Bodyweight ratios describe load relative to body size. Reference-force ratios describe demand relative to generic imported actuator force parameters; they are not measured strength utilization or activation. Group averages can hide high individual element ratios. No ratios or forces are capped.

For the 100 kg squat, peak quadriceps group tension is 14.81 kN per side: 17.16 bodyweights and 1.26 times its group reference. One vastus lateralis element reaches 2.13 times its reference. At the group peak, the right quadriceps supplies 386 Nm against 182 Nm from other muscles, leaving a 204 Nm net knee moment with a 2.61 cm effective arm. These values require anatomical and force-sharing validation; normalization does not validate them. A zero selected latissimus force does not establish actual inactivity.

Inputs, checks and remaining work
  • Squat descent 1.25 s; bottom hold 0.15 s; ascent 0.80 / 0.90 / 1.15 s for 60 / 100 / 140 kg. These are explicit illustrative tempos, not an inferred load-velocity law or personal 1RM.
  • The bar has an ideal attachment to the upper back. Translational inertia and a uniform-rod rotational inertia approximation are included. Hand pose is checked; hand/back contact-force sharing and pressure are not solved.
  • Rigid foot support: 2.5 cm heel elevation, 15° toe-out, 34 cm sole-reference stance width. Ground forces and COP are derived from full-body Newton–Euler balance and allocated inside friction cones. No root support actuator or strength clipping.
  • Checks cover force/moment balance, independent engine agreement, planted-foot drift, grasp frames, work–energy and joint-power identities. These verify the prescribed model; they do not validate an individual's squat technique or muscle recruitment.
  • Tempo sensitivity rescales time on the same path and rechecks the contact wrench. Cases that are infeasible for this planted-foot path are omitted from power estimates.
  • CMJ statistics cover lowest COM to takeoff. Squat statistics cover ascent. Display replays and holds are excluded.
  • Box 40 / 60 / 80 cm remains a separate dynamic task. Box height will not be converted directly into jump power or equivalent squat kilograms. Landing is not yet simulated.
  • Muscles use a whole-history conditional force-sharing solution. The time contours show nine selected groups with a common absolute-N palette. The side rendering retains the full shaft with plates omitted to expose posture; the main view shows the complete bar.
