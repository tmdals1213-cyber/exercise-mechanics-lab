Movement -> mechanics -> muscle tension

Motion definition -> contact and inverse dynamics -> conditional muscle tensions -> within-muscle comparison. No physiological activation, EMG, fatigue or injury predictions.

Primary sources reviewed 2026-11-23. Annotated links; full-text numerical extraction remains pending where stated.
Open-source 2D pose estimation library / current docs

RTMPose / RTMLib

code | pose_api | body_with_feet

Inputs: Video frames and athlete bounding boxes; Halpe26 includes toe and heel keypoints.

Outputs: 2D keypoints and confidence scores; no measured depth or forces.

Use here: Independent tracking check for the crouch/tuck phases where MediaPipe selected background legs. Installed only in the isolated reference-tracking environment.

Limits: Manual athlete boxes do not eliminate occlusion or keypoint errors. Estimated keypoints require frame review, and cannot establish calibrated 3D dynamics.

Open-source pipeline + peer-reviewed paper / 2023

OpenCap: Human movement dynamics from smartphone videos

paper | kinematics | dynamics | example

Inputs: Two or more synchronized, calibrated camera views; subject dimensions; a musculoskeletal model.

Outputs: 3D kinematics, modeled contacts, joint mechanics and conditional muscle-force estimates.

Use here: Separate video kinematics from dynamic simulation; inspect the kinetics example and contact/trajectory-tracking workflow.

Limits: Validation applies to the reported tasks and conditions. It does not validate our single-view box jump or 240 kg squat. Its physiological outputs are outside our present scope.

Open-source solver + peer-reviewed paper / 2020

OpenSim Moco: Musculoskeletal optimal control

paper | code | reproduction | example | tutorial

Inputs: Rigid-body model, muscle paths, external contacts, task goals and optionally a reference trajectory.

Outputs: Prescribed, tracked or predicted motions and muscle-driven solutions, depending on the formulation.

Use here: The squat-to-stand example is a concrete reference for movement definition, dynamic solution and comparing a model modification.

Limits: The teaching example is simplified, not a validated full-body loaded high-/low-bar/front squat. We would borrow the mechanics formulation without adding physiological activation or fatigue predictions.

Open-source tracking pipeline + software paper / 2022

Pose2Sim: An open-source Python package for multiview markerless kinematics

paper | code

Inputs: Calibrated multi-view video and 2D body observations.

Outputs: 3D marker trajectories and OpenSim kinematics.

Use here: Camera calibration, triangulation, tracking QC and musculoskeletal IK before any force calculation.

Limits: A kinematics workflow does not by itself identify contact forces or muscle tensions. Single-view depth ambiguity remains in our current source video.

Open-source fitting pipeline + peer-reviewed paper / 2023

AddBiomechanics: Automating model scaling, inverse kinematics, and inverse dynamics from human motion data through sequential optimization

paper | code | reproduction | docs

Inputs: Optical marker trajectories; corresponding measured ground-reaction forces for the dynamics fit.

Outputs: Scaled geometry, marker registration, kinematics, adjusted inertial properties and inverse dynamics.

Use here: Quantify root residuals and fit motion/inertia to measured forces rather than treating force balance alone as human-motion validation.

Limits: This is not a direct video-to-individual-muscle-force tool. Our video reference has no measured force plates, so its measured-force validation cannot be transferred.

Open-source model conversion and validation tools / current docs

MyoConverter

code | kinematics | kinetics | limitations

Inputs: OpenSim musculoskeletal models.

Outputs: MuJoCo model conversion and optimization of muscle path/force properties.

Use here: Compare muscle lengths, moment arms and force behavior across engines before trusting tension magnitudes.

Limits: Conversion has documented limitations for contacts, moving/conditional path points and some actuators. Successful conversion does not validate every muscle path or exercise.

tutorial

Peer-reviewed measured-motion + muscle-estimation comparison / 2024

A Biomechanical Comparison Between a High and Low Barbell Placement on Net Joint Moments, Kinematics, Muscle Forces, and Muscle-Specific Moments in 3 Repetition Maximum Back Squats

paper | doi

Inputs: Twelve trained men; standardized stance; high- and low-bar 3RM trials; motion capture and force plates.

Outputs: Inverse-dynamic joint moments and statically optimized individual muscle forces.

Use here: Closest empirical comparison to the proposed high-/low-bar muscle-tension study.

Limits: No significant lower-limb force/moment differences were found under this protocol. Do not force our model to produce a large style effect. Model-estimated muscle forces are not directly measured tensions. Abstract reviewed; numerical full-text tables not yet extracted.

Peer-reviewed depth/load mechanics study / 2012

Effect of squat depth and barbell load on relative muscular effort in squatting

paper | doi

Inputs: Ten strength-trained women; 50-90% 1RM; squat joint moments and angle-matched maximum voluntary torques.

Outputs: Joint-level relative muscular effort as net moment divided by angle-matched maximum torque.

Use here: Separate depth and load in the comparison design and match the mechanical quantity being compared.

Limits: RME is a joint torque ratio, not individual muscle activation or direct tendon tension. Its strength-normalized metric is outside our present output scope. Abstract reviewed.

Peer-reviewed front/back squat comparison / 2009

A biomechanical comparison of back and front squats in healthy trained individuals

paper | doi

Inputs: Fifteen trained participants; motion, force and surface EMG during front/back squats.

Outputs: Net knee forces and moments, with EMG as a separate measurement.

Use here: External bar placement, knee compression/shear and posture checks for the future front-squat branch.

Limits: Do not substitute its EMG for muscle tension or generalize a relative-load protocol to an equal-100 kg comparison without matching conditions. Abstract reviewed.
